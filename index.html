The Mooncake Festival

A story of the brightest, roundest moon of the whole year

Also called the Mid-Autumn Festival

Good morning, friends! Today we are going on a trip — no airplane needed — to a festival celebrated by families all around the world: the Mooncake Festival. It happens every fall, on the night when the moon is the brightest and roundest of the whole year. Look at our big friendly moon here. Have you ever seen the moon look this round? Keep that picture in your mind, because it will follow us through our whole story today.

What Is the Mooncake Festival?

  • It comes in fall, in September or October, on a special night
  • That night, the moon is the roundest and brightest of the year
  • Families celebrate it in China, Vietnam, Korea, and many other places
  • It is a happy time for family, food, stories, and moon-gazing
Let's learn the name first. In China, people call it the Mid-Autumn Festival, because it comes right in the middle of autumn. In Vietnam, it is a big party just for children, called Tet Trung Thu. In Korea, families celebrate Chuseok. Different names — but always the same big, bright moon. Now raise your hand: does anyone here celebrate a special fall day at home with family? Every answer is welcome in our class.

Look at the Moon!

On festival night, the moon looks like a perfect circle — round like a family sitting together in a ring.

Think: what round things can you find in our classroom?

Here is our star of the show — the moon! On the night of the festival, the moon looks like a giant, glowing circle in the sky. A circle has no corners and no edges — nothing sticking out. That is why the round moon means something special: being together, with no one left out. Now it's your turn: look around our classroom. What round things can you find? Two or three answers, then we fly up to the moon for a story.

A Story From the Moon

1

Long, long ago, a kind woman named Chang'e floated all the way up to the moon.

2

A friendly white rabbit hopped up too, so she would never be lonely.

3

Some people say you can still see them up there — look closely tonight!

Every festival has a story, and this one lives on the moon. People have told it for thousands of years. A kind woman named Chang'e floated up to the moon, and a friendly white rabbit hopped along so she would never be lonely. When the moon is full, some people say you can see the shapes of the lady and her rabbit in the moon's soft spots. This is a legend — a very old story that families love to tell. Tonight, squint at the moon and see what shapes YOU find.

Mooncakes: A Sweet Round Treat

  • Round like the moonThe shape means family, together in a circle
  • Sweet insideLotus seed paste, red bean, or sweet dates
  • A hidden moonSome have a golden egg yolk inside — a tiny moon!
  • Pretty on topStamped with flowers, rabbits, and lucky words
Now for the tastiest part — mooncakes! Say it with me: moon-cake. A mooncake is about the size of your hand, round like the full moon, and filled with something sweet. Some families cut ONE mooncake into enough wedges for the whole family — everyone gets a piece, because sharing is the whole point. And look closely at some mooncakes: inside hides a golden egg yolk, like a tiny moon hiding in your treat. Would you try a bite? What flavor sounds best to you?

Let me introduce you to Mei — a kid just like you, who celebrates this festival every year. Her night goes like this: first, family dinner, with Grandma and Grandpa at the table. Then the best part — lanterns! Kids carry glowing lanterns shaped like fish, stars, and rabbits, and walk to the park together. After that, story time under the moon, and finally — mooncakes for everyone. Now tell me: which part of Mei's night would YOU like best? Point to it — dinner, lanterns, story, or cake?

One Moon for Everyone

“

When you look up at the moon tonight, remember: someone you love — near or far — may be looking at that very same moon.

— A Mid-Autumn saying

Here is the most beautiful part of the whole festival. Everyone on Earth looks up at the same moon. The very same moon your grandma sees, the same one a cousin far away sees, the same one a friend across the ocean sees. So on festival night, families look up and feel close to each other, even when they are far apart. Maybe some of your neighbors or classmates celebrated last night. Tonight, you can look up too — and think of someone you love while you do it.

No mooncake? Any soft ball works — imagination included.

Time to play a festival game! It works just like pass-the-parcel, but with a mooncake. We make a big circle — round like the moon, remember. When the music starts, we pass the mooncake around. When the music stops, whoever is holding it shares one thing they love to share with their family — a food, a game, a story, anything. If we don't have a mooncake, any soft ball will do. Ready? Let's make our circle nice and round!

One more time, all together: Zhongqiu Jie! Happy Mid-Autumn Festival, friends!
